
# Week 1: Threat vs Vulnerability

## 1. Introduction

Cybersecurity is important to protect computer systems, networks, and sensitive information. Threats and vulnerabilities are two important concepts in cybersecurity. A threat is a potential danger that can cause harm, while a vulnerability is a weakness that can be exploited by a threat.

## 2. Objectives

- To understand the difference between threats and vulnerabilities.
- To identify threats and vulnerabilities in different scenarios.
- To explain the reasons for classifying each scenario.

## 3. Comparison Between Threats and Vulnerabilities

| No. | Aspect | Threat | Vulnerability |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Definition | A potential cause of harm to a system, network, or data. | A weakness in a system, application, or security control that can be exploited. |
| 2 | Source | May originate from cybercriminals, malware, or malicious insiders. | May arise from software flaws, weak passwords, or improper configurations. |
| 3 | Nature | Represents a potential danger to information security. | Represents a weakness that may expose a system to security risks. |
| 4 | Examples | Phishing attacks, ransomware, and unauthorized access attempts. | Unpatched software, weak passwords, and improperly exposed network ports. |
| 5 | Impact | Can cause data breaches, financial losses, or service disruption. | Can increase the likelihood of successful cyberattacks. |
| 6 | Identification | Identified through threat intelligence, security monitoring, and analysis of attack activities. | Identified through vulnerability scanning, security audits, and penetration testing. |
| 7 | Mitigation | Managed through threat detection, firewalls, and incident response measures. | Reduced by applying security patches, strengthening authentication, and correcting misconfigurations. |
| 8 | Role in an Attack | May exploit a weakness or use another attack method to cause harm. | Provides an opportunity for a threat to compromise a system when exploitable. |

## 4. Real World scenario

| No | Scenario | Category | Reasoning |
|----|----------|----------|-----------|
| 1 | An employee receives an email from “HR Department” asking to verify their login details via a link. | Threat | Phishing — an external malicious attempt to trick a user into revealing credentials. |
| 2 | A company server is running Windows Server 2012, which no longer receives security updates. | Vulnerability | Outdated/unpatched software — exploitable weakness due to lack of updates. |
| 3 | A developer accidentally uploads API keys to a public GitHub repository. | Vulnerability | Sensitive information exposure due to misconfiguration or human error. |
| 4 | A hacker uses a botnet to flood a website with traffic until it becomes unavailable. | Threat | DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) — an active external attack. |
| 5 | The firewall in a branch office is misconfigured to allow all inbound traffic. | Vulnerability | Weak configuration that could be exploited. |
| 6 | An insider copies confidential customer data onto a personal USB drive. | Threat | Insider threat — intentional malicious activity by an authorized person. |
| 7 | The organization’s password policy allows users to set “12345” as their password. | Vulnerability | Weak access control due to poor policy enforcement. |
| 8 | Cybercriminals send ransomware through email attachments to multiple employees. | Threat | Ransomware — a direct cyberattack designed to harm and demand payment. |
| 9 | A company website doesn’t validate user input in its contact form, allowing malicious SQL commands. | Vulnerability | SQL Injection risk — a coding flaw in input validation. |
| 10 | Attackers use fake job advertisements on social media to trick users into sharing personal data. | Threat | Social engineering — psychological manipulation used to gain sensitive information. |

## 5. Conclusion

In conclusion, threats and vulnerabilities are important concepts in cybersecurity. A threat is something that can cause harm to a system, while a vulnerability is a weakness that attackers can take advantage of. For example, a hacker may try to access a system, and a weak password can make it easier for the attack to succeed.By identifying and understanding both threats and vulnerabilities, organizations can implement stronger **security controls, policies, and defense mechanisms** to protect their digital assets and maintain the **confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA)** of their information systems.
